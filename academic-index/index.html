SDA Compass

One read on fit, across every axis. SDA Compass brings together the recruiting Academic Index, athletic strength, and student profile, then measures your athlete against each target school so you see exactly where they stand and what it would take.

01 · The idea

One index, built from every axis of recruiting.

Recruiting is not decided by grades alone. SDA Compass reads the two axes coaches actually weigh, and layers the student profile on top, then reports the result school by school.

Most tools stop at academics. SDA Compass treats a recruit as a whole picture. Academics set the bar a coach has to clear to bring an athlete in. Athletic strength determines how far a coach is willing to reach to do it. The student profile of leadership, research, and activities rounds out the case.

The important part is that none of this is judged in the abstract. Every number is measured against a specific school, because a profile that is a clear yes at one program can be a genuine reach at a more selective one. SDA Compass gives you that read for each school on your list, in plain language.

03 · The academic axis

The Academic Index, and its bands.

The academic input to SDA Compass is the real recruiting Academic Index. Course rigor, essays, and activities matter to admissions, but they sit outside this particular number.

How it's calculated

The Academic Index runs on a 60 to 240 scale, built from two inputs. An unweighted GPA is mapped onto a 20 to 80 band, and an SAT total contributes on a 40 to 160 scale, roughly twice the pull of the GPA piece. An ACT is converted to its SAT equivalent first, and where a school still ranks its students, class rank can stand in for GPA.

Because the Ivies are test optional, we calculate the index two ways, once with the test score and once without, and lead with whichever is higher. A strong GPA paired with a modest test score sometimes scores better without the test, and knowing that is the difference between applying with the score and applying without it. The exact weighting has never been published, so every version of this number is a reconstruction. Ours follows the standard convention, and we stay honest about what it can and cannot tell you.

NESCAC A, B, and C bands

NESCAC schools do not use a single cutoff. They sort recruits into an A, B, or C band relative to that school's own admitted class, and each team gets a limited number of slots in each band. The consequence that matters most is that the same athlete can be an A band at one school and a B band at a more selective one, because the bar moves with the school.

An A band athlete is on par with the school's typical admit and clears the bar largely on academics. A B band sits just below and needs a coach to spend a slot. A C band is meaningfully below and is reserved for the athletes a coach most wants, which is why those slots are scarce. As a rule of thumb, a recruit should generally sit within about one standard deviation of the school's academic profile to be supportable.

04 · The two axes

How academics and athletics meet.

The band is the gate. Athletic strength is the lever. What decides a recruit is how those two meet, school by school.

The rule is simple. The lower the band, the stronger the athlete has to be for a coach to justify the slot. That single relationship is what SDA Compass makes visible.

It also means the same result changes the odds differently depending on the band. An A band athlete clears the bar on academics alone, so athletics is a bonus, and a modest result does not drag them down. A B band athlete sits just below the class, and this is where athletics swings the outcome, with a strong result pulling them up and a weak one leaving them exposed. A C band athlete is far enough below that only a coach's top priority earns one of the few available slots.

This is the read most families never get. Two athletes with identical academics can have completely different odds at the same school depending on their athletic tier, and the same athlete can be a clear yes at one school and a reach at another. SDA Compass shows both axes at once, for every school on the list.
